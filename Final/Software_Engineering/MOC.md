## Year 11

### Overview
- [[Year_11_Software_Engineering_Summary]]: condensed guide to all three Year 11 focus areas, with exam-focused examples and a revision checklist

### 1. [[Programming_Fundamentals]]
Software development steps, requirements vs specifications, error types, testing methods, conversion methods, Waterfall vs Agile, control structures, subroutines, **desk checks with full worked trace tables**, DFDs, structure charts, data dictionaries, decision trees, storyboards, Gantt charts, numbering systems, two's complement, and the four programming paradigms.

### 2. [[Object_Oriented_Programming]]
The four pillars, classes and objects, message passing, design diagrams (DFD, structure chart, class diagram), the **DFD → class → method → attribute pipeline**, design approaches (top-down, bottom-up, façade, agility), the ten algorithm effectiveness criteria, and collaboration factors.

### 3. [[Mechatronics]]
CPUs vs microcontrollers, the registers and **fetch–execute cycle**, machine language, sensors and actuators, wiring diagrams, control algorithms, open vs closed loop, on/off vs proportional vs PID, and electricity fundamentals.

## Year 12

### Overview
- [[Year_12_Software_Engineering_Summary]]: definitions grouped under the three Year 12 topics; no cross-topic glossary.

### 4. [[Programming_For_The_Web]]
Client vs server-side, networking hardware and topologies, protocols and ports, the six V's of big data, web mining, W3C and accessibility, **CSS syntax**, version control, MVC and CMSes, **SQL and ORM**, the TLS handshake, encryption types, digital signatures, XSS, web storage, and the Caesar cipher.

### 5. [[Software_Automation]]
AI vs ML, the four ML types, **the three stages of MLOps**, best-use guidance for linear, polynomial, logistic and KNN regression (plus decision-tree regression), neural networks, decision trees and overfitting, psychology links, and the ethics of automation.

### 6. [[Secure_Software_Architecture]]
Security by design, the **six fundamental security concepts**, security across the SDLC, secure coding practices, common vulnerabilities, validation and sanitisation, the five testing strategies, the SSA1 Fernet and SHA-256 examples, RBAC and MFA, and the Australian privacy context.

## The Verbs

| Verb | What to do |
|---|---|
| **List / State** | Name items — no explanation |
| **Define** | State the meaning and essential qualities |
| **Describe** | Provide characteristics and features |
| **Explain** | Relate cause and effect; say why and how |
| **Analyse** | Identify components and relationships; draw out implications |
| **Justify** | Support an argument with evidence |
| **Evaluate** | Make a judgement based on criteria |
